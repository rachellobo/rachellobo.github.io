<em>Rachel Lobo (2026)</em>

## Abstract
This paper considers causal inference in models where treatment and comparison groups have unequal treatment statuses in the pre-period, or ``unequal baseline'' treatment. A simple inversion of the Difference-in-Differences (DiD) model fails to identify treatment effects when treatment effects evolve over time, as is the case in many empirical settings. I explore an unequal baseline model with binary treatment and propose a causal inference approach that incorporates dynamic treatment effects. When level outcomes between Switchers and Stayers converge in the post-treatment period, treatment effects are estimable using simple methods and under mild assumptions. I prove that causal inference under this approach is valid for complex potential outcomes models such as interactive fixed effects. I target two parameters: the total treatment effect and the (instantaneous) extensive margin treatment effect. The event study estimation strategy I propose is easy to implement and provides an empirical test of the research design. I apply the methods derived in this paper to examine the role of the Pregnancy Discrimination Act of 1978 on women's employment.
